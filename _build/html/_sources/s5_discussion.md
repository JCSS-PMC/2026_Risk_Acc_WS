# Conclusions and Outlook – Closing of the Workshop and Written Contributions

Session 5 · Thursday 22 October 2026, 12.38–12.40

## Closing

The discussions after each presentation had been so lively on both days that the workshop ran well behind its schedule throughout – "as always", as the JCSS President remarked in the chat. When the last presentation ended at 12.38, past the planned end of the workshop and with the JCSS meeting due to start after lunch, the open discussion foreseen for Session 5 could not be held as a separate session. Instead:

- Jochen Köhler announced that the workshop would be documented in proceedings – collecting all presentations together with a written text on the discussions – and invited all participants, in the room and online, to send written comments, afterthoughts on their presentation or on the discussion, to him and Arnold Yuan.
- The conclusions drawn from the presentations and discussions are given on the [Conclusions](./conclusions.md) page; they were drafted after the workshop from the recording and are open for comment.
- The topic was to be taken up again in the JCSS meeting that followed (22–23 October 2026) – among others the closer look at individual risk announced in Session 3 – and in the work of the JCSS towards the Probabilistic Model Code and the revision of ISO 2394.

The workshop closed with the group photograph in front of the Engineering building, and lunch.

> I think Colin Caprani is correct in telling not to forget others who would lose when limited resources are used to (further) improve safety elsewhere. ## Contributions from the online chat not taken up in the room

- **Benjamin Schmid** (during Session 3): Since 2011, the principle of life-saving costs, or the efficiency of measures (ALARP), has been firmly established in the current Swiss maintenance standard SIA 269, and this will now also be implemented in the National Annexes to SN EN 1990 (second generation) for both new and existing buildings.
- **Nadieh Meinen** (SDU; during Session 1, on Alex Cao's presentation): Is the LQI framework mainly intended for decision making for populations of structures, or also at the level of individual structures? – Jochen Köhler (chat): also for individual structures, but then the infinite-renewal assumption does not hold any more and the objective function needs to be reformulated. – Jelle Knibbe (chat): their recent journal paper covers an individual structure but still uses the renewal model for simplicity; in a more thorough case study a formulation with a set time span could be considered. – Nadieh Meinen: even for an individual structure a new one is often needed; the question is then which reliability level the replacement is designed to – if designed semi-probabilistically, should that reliability level be adopted in the optimisation?
- **Ton Vrouwenvelder** (chat, Session 1): Is there a report or paper with the derivation of the equations (Alex Cao)? – The paper is not finished; the speaker intends to share it.
- **Jelle Knibbe** (chat, Session 1, on the discussion of failure costs in the minimum criterion): the reasoning is that the reduction of economic loss should not factor into the societal minimum safety.
- **Farid Vafadar** (chat, Session 3): Are the reliability targets defined for individual members or for the whole building? (answered in the room, see [Session 3](./s3_koehler.md)). How have the environmental risks been considered (Netherlands)? (answered in the room, see [Risk Acceptance in the Netherlands](./s3_steenbergen.md)).
- **Benjamin Schmid** (chat): Will the presentations – or even the video of the workshop – be made available later? – Jochen Köhler: the workshop will be documented afterwards (these pages).
